# Brief: The 98¢ Trade, "What you get" section

Self-authored under explicit creative delegation. Tobia pointed at the section
and said: "animate it, make it cooler, maybe some 3D shape model of some fun
product (keep it professional), maybe a small model of Jev, or the product
itself, maybe making it creative", then "go!". Not interviewed.

Scope: one section of an existing page, not a new site. The page around it
keeps its grammar (Construct: paper, ink, clay, cut-paper marks).

1. **Vibe:** professional, tactile, a little playful. Cut paper made physical.
2. **Journey (this section):** runs itself → the edge → you get it → you build on it.
3. **Energy:** calm hero above, this section is the one that moves, calm chart after.
4. **Feeling by stage:** ease (it turns on its own), the "oh" (the 2¢ lifts out),
   clarity (98¢ + 2¢, face on), ambition (a stack rises under it).
   **Peak:** the slice lifting out of the coin. "The coin literally gives up its
   last 2¢, and that's the whole trade."
5. **The thing no other site does:** the product's own mark comes apart to
   explain the product.
6. **Range:** premium-minimal with the site's cut-paper warmth.
7. **Structure:** one pinned stage; copy states cross over inside it.
8. **Assets:** none generated. The 3D object is built from the mark's own
   outlines in specimens.tsx (DISC_CUT, SLICE, DISC), so the 2D and 3D mark
   cannot drift apart. Not a Jev character: Jev is TypeSafe's brand, and
   inventing a mascot for it would be putting words in another company's mouth.

**Tell-someone:** "It's the page where the coin gives up its last 2¢ to show
you what you're buying."

**Why the old section went:** four identical heading-plus-text cells in a grid,
the most recognisable AI-page tell there is.

**Reduced motion:** no pin, no WebGL; the flat mark and all four points, plain.
