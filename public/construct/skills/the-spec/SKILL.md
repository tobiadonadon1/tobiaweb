# The spec

A PRD is a decision document, not a novel and not a Jira export. One sitting. If it takes a week, you are writing two products.

## Produce, in this order

1. Name. What we call it when talking to each other.
2. Problem. Who hurts, how, how often. One paragraph. No TAM slides.
3. Who it is for, and who it is not for. Named, not "users".
4. Done when. Tests a stranger could run. Not "feels right".
5. Not this. The tempting extras that would wreck the sitting. Write them down so they stop sneaking in.
6. Surfaces. Screens, APIs, emails, empty states. Each in one line plus the job it does.
7. Risks. The three ways this fails in the real world, and the cheapest check for each.
8. Out of scope until later. Dated if you can.

Then stop. Do not add architecture unless a decision cannot wait.

## Rules

- No invented metrics. If you do not have a number, write the question instead.
- No "phase 2" dumping ground that is actually the real product.
- Every surface names an owner of the decision (even if that owner is the person in the room).
- If two people would build different things from a sentence, the sentence is not done.

## When this is not the skill

- Atelier, page-copy, motion-scale. Those are how it looks, reads, and moves after the spec exists.
- A bug list, a QA pass (tester), or a launch checklist (ship-check).
- A 40-page strategy deck. Cut until it fits in one sitting.

## Test

Hand it to someone who was not in the room. If they ask "what do we actually ship on Friday?", you are not finished.
